\section{Discussion, Practical Implications, and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
\subsection{Discussion and Implications for SE Practice}
\noindent\textbf{1) Beyond localization. Why broader coverage yields modest repair gains.}
Showing an investigator the relevant function creates a review opportunity, but does not supply the missing security contract. Our funnel makes that distinction explicit (Table~\ref{tab:engineering}, Panel B). Change-informed planning includes reference-vulnerable symbols in 366/387 attempts, compared with 52/387 primary SAST overlaps. Yet DeepSeek C3 falls from 252 localized hypotheses to 97 supported diagnoses, and Gemini C3 falls from 235 to 23. Coverage therefore moves the bottleneck into explaining why the current implementation violates a boundary. Retained edits can still address a local symptom while leaving the wider contract broken. The semantic review identifies lexical path normalization without symlink containment, validation that overlooks inherited connection fields, and size checks performed after decompression allocates memory (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}). These inspected failures concern relationships among representations, consumers, and lifecycle stages. A review agent needs to turn localization into explicit obligations about those relationships, then test the proposed repair at the boundary where the property matters. A useful investigation record should identify the incoming representation, the relevant consumer, and the expected rejection or preservation behavior. That makes disagreement actionable. A reviewer can inspect whether validation applies before an allocation or after decoding, instead of accepting a generic assertion that the function is now safe.

\noindent\textbf{2) Auditing intent alongside syntax. Preserve development explanations.}
A pull request that retains only the final diff loses the intermediate decisions and stated assumptions behind the implementation. C2's highest SecPass count with both investigators suggests that these explanations deserve preservation as review evidence (Table~\ref{tab:results}). Structural and textual history offer different clues. A guard never written cannot appear as a removed AST node, although changes to sibling functions may expose its omission. Recorded rationale can directly state an assumption about sanitization, caller guarantees, or library behavior that an investigator can check against current code. This does not require accepting the explanation as faithful internal reasoning~\cite{turpin,lanham}. It requires treating the statement as an auditable hypothesis. The lower C3 counts also motivate selective retrieval. Combined histories may dilute relevant evidence within a bounded context, but our study does not isolate that cause or control context length and attention. C1--C3 also include audit cues. The practical recommendation is to preserve symbol-linked explanations with a change and retrieve the excerpts that bear on a concrete unresolved obligation. Capture should retain the observable explanation and its associated action, rather than reconstructing missing reasoning afterward. Keeping Git changes and agent explanations separately attributable also lets teams adopt the workflow in repositories with incomplete historical traces. The absence of rationale should remain visible instead of being filled with a new model-generated narrative.

\noindent\textbf{3) From silent fixes to executable security memory.}
A repair's defensive purpose may not remain explicit in its surrounding code, as silent-fix research illustrates~\cite{colefunda}. Later maintenance can then challenge the same assumption under a new feature request~\cite{undoingfix}. \tracer{} externalizes that purpose in \code{edit_note.json}, linking the repaired-symbol hash, diagnostic assumption, invariant, and independently owned boundary check. The structural-biology study supplies identical documentation-stripped code to both conditions, making the note available separately to Memory+Gate. Joint success rises from 3/5 to 5/5 under this guarded workflow, including one feedback retry. The additional successes complete archive-format support and correct checkpoint exception handling while preserving saved checks. They do not establish that two vulnerabilities were reintroduced and prevented. For teams, the useful deliverable is an executable maintenance obligation that survives changes in control flow, with successful feature delivery distinguished from retaining the old secure version. The check must remain independently owned during the proposed edit, or an agent could weaken the acceptance condition alongside the code. If a feature legitimately changes the contract, the team must review that contract change explicitly. A passing saved check establishes preservation of its tested property, not compatibility with every future input.

\noindent\textbf{4) Engineering guardrails for review runners.}
Exploration and final validation require separate capacity. In the development recovery check, 68 of seventy discarded candidates exhaust their two functional-check slots or reach the turn cap before validation, while two miss a probe rerun. Local reevaluation finds 39 functional passes and one new security conversion. Reserving a final validation opportunity prevents scheduling alone from discarding a candidate, without treating that candidate as correct. Exact-hash reuse avoids repeating checks on unchanged patches. Runners should also distinguish diagnostic refutations from provider failures, preserving labels such as Gemini's \code{blockReason: OTHER} without inferring their cause. Together with retained patches and recognized terminal summaries, these records let a team separate review quality, protocol losses, and service reliability before deciding which component needs improvement. Candidate generation, patch retention, and official evaluation should therefore be separately observable states. This separation allows local tests to resume after a service interruption without charging for another investigation, while incomplete execution remains distinguishable from a failing assertion. It also prevents a successful tool call from being mistaken for a completed repair.

\subsection{Threats to Validity and Scope}
\emph{Construct and internal validity.} Reference-patch overlap measures review opportunity. Broad classes and incidental reference edits can overstate how precisely an investigator identifies the violated property. Task tests cover specified contracts, not whole-repository security or unrelated alerts. Test adequacy and overfitting remain limits~\cite{repairsurvey,overfitting}, and recorded rationale need not faithfully describe internal reasoning~\cite{turpin,lanham}. Repeated inspection of development failures changes several protocol mechanisms together, so the progression does not isolate their individual effects. C0 receives patch-selected targets, C1--C3 add audit cues without a cue-only control, and temporal evidence excludes upstream ancestry. Resumed execution retains earlier revisions, with paired original/candidate evaluations after environment recovery. The previously protected stratum is separately reported but is not an untouched prospective replication. Its smaller C2 gains over C0 constrain the strength of generalization from the development-exposed cases. A future prospective comparison should freeze the protocol and retrieval policy before examining the new tasks.

\emph{External and domain-study validity.} Selection favors Python outputs with publisher-reported functional success. The 42 admissions without complete shared outcomes may differ systematically in environment difficulty or provider reliability. Generator attempts share tasks, which motivates task-cluster intervals, while the small number of conversions limits their precision. The fifteen semantic reviews were selected to explain retained repair failures and rely on a single reviewer. Their proportions should not be generalized to all vulnerabilities. The component study covers five selected repairs in six structural-biology repositories. It is researcher-run with industry input, rather than an independent CVE verification exercise or practitioner deployment. Memory+Gate includes a retry absent from No Memory, so its gain cannot be attributed to memory alone. Source and check hashes make the evidence traceable, but do not independently establish semantic correctness. Broader repository coverage and independent practitioner validation remain future work.

\section{Related Work}
Repository-level evaluation must distinguish resolving a requested issue from enforcing its security conditions. SWE-bench measures issue resolution~\cite{swebench}, whereas SusVibes separately evaluates functional and security outcomes~\cite{susvibes,susvibesartifact}. CVEfixes links vulnerabilities to fixing histories, and Vul4J provides reproducible vulnerabilities and triggering tests~\cite{cvefixes,vul4j}. SWE-agent studies repository interaction interfaces~\cite{sweagent}, while ReAct supplies interleaved explanation/action traces~\cite{react}. For security investigation, PAGENT combines static guidance with dynamic profiling~\cite{pagent}, IRIS infers taint specifications and filters candidate paths~\cite{iris}, LLMDFA couples model reasoning with parsing and feasibility checks~\cite{llmdfa}, and RepoAudit explores data flows using memory and validation~\cite{repoaudit}. These approaches ground investigation in the implementation and its executable behavior. Our comparison asks what development evidence adds to a common current-source repair protocol, with the same scanner context and public validation interface available to every configuration. The question concerns the evidence supplied to an investigator rather than a replacement security oracle.

Historical analysis provides several complementary ways to recover security-relevant context from software changes. ViolationTracker reconstructs static-warning histories~\cite{violationtracker}, CoLeFunDa represents function changes to identify silent fixes~\cite{colefunda}, KNighter synthesizes checkers from historical patches~\cite{knighter}, and FixMiner extracts reusable repair patterns~\cite{fixminer}. GumTree provides structural edit matching~\cite{gumtree,gumtree3}. Analyzer-guided repair includes InferFix~\cite{inferfix}, while ChatRepair uses conversational test feedback~\cite{chatrepair} and RepairAgent autonomously inspects, edits, and tests code~\cite{repairagent}. These methods motivate our use of program evidence and feedback. Our evaluation additionally follows how candidates move from that evidence through supported diagnosis to a retained, independently evaluated repair.

For subsequent maintenance, Reflexion retains verbal feedback in episodic memory~\cite{reflexion}, Self-Refine iterates through model-generated feedback~\cite{selfrefine}, and POSTURA describes a persistent security graph~\cite{postura}. The exploratory \emph{Undoing the Fix} report studies deliberately elicited regressions under an AI-control setting~\cite{undoingfix}. Our maintenance study uses benign feature requests to exercise an earlier repair invariant through independently owned executable checks. Recording first proposals, feedback-assisted acceptance, and secure fallback separately makes the workflow's protection and its feature-delivery cost observable.

\section{Conclusion}
\tracer{} links change-informed planning, temporal AST evolution, recorded rationale, and guarded repair to persistent maintenance evidence. Across 3,096 cells, C2 achieves the highest shared-cohort SecPass counts with both investigators, reaching 76/345 with DeepSeek and 75/345 with Gemini from 68 original passes. Stage-level losses explain why broad review coverage produces modest repair gains. In structural-biology components, memory with a one-retry gate completes five feature evolutions while preserving saved boundaries, compared with three under unguarded evolution. The portable skill and archived evidence support auditable security review and maintenance.
